\begin{abstract}

Healthcare insurance fraud is a significant challenge that can lead to financial losses and affect the efficiency and reliability of healthcare systems. In this project, we propose an AI-based healthcare claims fraud detection system that combines medical validation with supervised machine learning and Explainable AI. The system uses NFHS-5 health survey data to identify clinically inconsistent patterns such as reported medical conditions without corresponding treatment or medication, and uses Medicare healthcare claims data for provider-level potential fraud detection. For the claims analysis, provider-level features including total claims, total reimbursement, average reimbursement, inpatient and outpatient claim statistics, and unique patients are extracted and used to train classification models such as Random Forest, Logistic Regression, and XGBoost. To improve transparency and interpretability, Explainable AI techniques using SHAP are employed to understand the contribution of individual features to model predictions. The proposed approach provides a data-driven framework for identifying potentially fraudulent healthcare providers while incorporating medical consistency checks and interpretable machine learning. The system can support analysts and investigators by prioritizing suspicious cases for further review rather than replacing human decision-making.

\end{abstract}
